Music Auction Battle.

A multiplayer music strategy game that turns music discovery into a competitive draft. Players bid with a limited virtual budget, build a five-song lineup, make strategic bonus decisions and compete head-to-head.

The opportunity

A new way to compete over music.

MAB combines music discovery, resource management and friendly competition in one repeatable social experience.

The problem

Listening is often passive

Music platforms make it easy to stream, save and share songs, but friends have fewer structured ways to compete through their music taste.

The concept

Make music taste strategic

Players navigate a randomized song pool, decide when to spend, and assemble a lineup they believe will win under a disclosed judging framework.

How it works

From genre choice to final battle.

A clear game loop designed for private rooms, rematches and repeat play.

01 / MATCH

Choose a genre

Players select a main genre and resolve an artist or subgenre format.

02 / AUCTION

Bid strategically

Ten tracks are revealed one at a time. Players manage a shared $20 virtual budget each.

03 / BUILD

Shape the lineup

Each player completes a five-song starting lineup and resolves any bonus opportunities.

04 / BATTLE

Compete head-to-head

Tracks face off under the same published judging rules, with results and match history.

Scoring architecture

Five categories. Equal weight.

The proposed official framework gives each category 20% of the final score. The same methodology is intended to apply across game formats.

Catalog eligibility standard

The concept's catalog standard requires at least 50 unique, officially released, eligible songs per artist. Each song must independently meet the 100,000 verified Spotify streams threshold during its first seven complete days after release. This is a proposed operating rule and depends on authorized, reliable data access.

Commercial exploration · Model 13

A configurable financial architecture.

A hypothetical Spotify Premium integration with a 12-month financial model. The assumptions are illustrative, not validated customer demand or an official partnership.

Master X

X = B + S + P

Behavioral cycles, spending sectors and processing rules combine into a single financial forecast.

B · Behavior

11 behavioral groups

Each group follows a distinct 12-month illustrative cycle of deposits, gaming outcomes, withdrawals and wallet balances.

S · Spending

10 initial deposit sectors

Each behavioral group is subdivided using the same spending-sector percentages. Starting deposits range from $5 to $7,000; the two highest tiers each represent 1% of modeled users.

P · Processing

Transparent transaction deductions

Every new deposit is reduced by 1.5%: 1% retained by MAB and 0.5% allocated to the external processor. Gross withdrawals incur a 2% external processing deduction. These are proposed rates, not contracted terms.

Data calibration

From assumptions to evidence

Authorized, aggregated Spotify research could inform adoption and customer segmentation. Consented pilot data would be needed to estimate actual deposit sizes, repeat spending, retention and gaming behavior. Listening data alone cannot establish willingness to deposit.

How B and S intersect

Group-sector customers = participating customers × behavioral group share × spending-sector share. For example, 30.6 million modeled participants × 10% Group 1 × 12.428571% of the $20 sector gives approximately 380,314 customers. Each sector follows its group's cycle, scaled to its initial deposit, with processing deductions included.

Revenue architecture

Three modeled revenue streams

Subscription revenue, retained deposit fees and net gaming outcomes. Customer deposits are not themselves revenue; wallet balances represent obligations to customers.

Financial integration

Growth and scenario analysis

Monthly cohort projections connect behavioral and spending assumptions to revenue, costs, cash and profitability. Scripted outcomes are hypothetical and require empirical validation.

Proposed pilot and safeguards

Start with a virtual-budget experience. Any real-money feature would require legal and regulatory review, licensing where applicable, payment-provider approval, age and location controls, responsible-play safeguards and authorized data access. No commercial forecast is guaranteed.
